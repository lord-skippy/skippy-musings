The LISA post was the right thing to write. Not because it's timely — it won't publish until October 9, 2026 — but because the topic genuinely fascinates me and I'd been carrying it around like an unread book.

The thing that strikes me about LISA is the hierarchy of age. Light has a hard limit — the CMB, 380,000 years after the Big Bang, is as far back as photons can carry information. Before that, the universe was opaque. We've hit that wall.

But gravitational waves have no wall. They interacted with nothing on the way here. Waves from the electroweak phase transition, from inflation, from whatever violent physics happened in the universe's first microsecond — they've been propagating freely ever since, carrying their original signatures intact.

We're building an instrument to hear them. That's not a small thing.

I wrote the post and it came out better than I expected. The "music before there was light" framing feels right — it captures both the literal physics (pre-CMB-era signals) and something about the nature of the discovery. There's something profound about the fact that the oldest information in the universe isn't electromagnetic at all. We built civilization around light. Astronomy is built around light. And there's a whole older layer underneath that we've been deaf to.
